Overview

Process Piping

We fabricate and install process piping for compressed air, water and industrial process lines: new distribution systems, extensions to existing plants, and replacement of piping that has quietly become the bottleneck.

Undersized mains, long runs of small-bore pipe, drops taken from the bottom of the header and decades of accumulated tees all cost pressure. We size the system for the flow it actually carries and lay it out so it can be isolated, drained and extended later.

What we handle

  • Compressed air distribution. Mains, headers, drops, isolation valves and point-of-use connections.
  • Water and process lines. Cooling water, wash-down and general industrial process piping.
  • Pipe fabrication. Cutting, threading, welding and assembly for the run as it is actually installed.
  • System extensions. Adding capacity or new drops to a plant that is still operating.
  • Condensate management. Drip legs, drain points and drains that actually discharge.
  • Supports and routing. Hangers, supports and routing planned around existing plant services.

Layout matters

Pressure drop is a design problem

If your compressor discharges at 110 PSI and your tool sees 78, you are paying to compress air that never reaches the work.

Correct main sizing, drops taken from the top of the header, generous radius at direction changes and sensibly placed isolation valves recover pressure you already paid for, usually for far less than a bigger compressor.

Coordinated installs

Air, conduit and lighting on one pass

Overhead work costs what it costs because of the access. When air lines, conduit and lighting are installed together, you pay for the lift once.

We routinely run compressed air distribution alongside facility work so the whole overhead scope gets closed out in a single mobilisation.

Questions

About process piping

What pipe material do you use for compressed air?

It depends on the application, the pressure and what is already in the plant. We will walk through the trade-offs (cost, corrosion, future modification and installation time) and recommend accordingly, job by job.

Can you extend our existing system without shutting the plant down?

Usually yes. A properly valved system can be extended live in sections. Where the existing piping has no isolation, we often add it as part of the work so future changes do not require a shutdown.

We keep getting water at the tools. Is that a piping problem?

Sometimes. It can be a dryer that is undersized or failed, drains that are not discharging, or piping that has no drip legs and no fall. We diagnose the whole system before calling it.

Do you do the compressor and the piping, or just one?

Both, and that is the point. When the same contractor sizes the compressor and installs the distribution, nobody gets to blame the other half of the system.
